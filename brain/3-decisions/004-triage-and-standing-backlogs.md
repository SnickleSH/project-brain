# 004. Split routing from synthesis; add standing backlogs

**Context** — The only path from inbox to action ran through `/brain:plan`,
which does two jobs at once: routing material (facts to reference, design to
architecture) and synthesizing a design doc. `/brain:breakdown` requires an
architecture doc and `/brain:execute` requires a checklist linked to one. So
shallow work that simply arrived — "set up GA4 on the landing page" — could
only escape the inbox by having an architecture doc invented for it, and then
a checklist derived from that doc. In a demand-driven repo this produced
per-task artifacts at volume: exactly the granularity violation the contract
forbids, generated by the contract's own happy path.

**Decision** — We will separate the two jobs. `/brain:triage` dispatches the
inbox without synthesizing, classifying **per item rather than per note** into
FACT → reference, TASK → a standing backlog, LOG → a `brain/log/` stream,
DESIGN → left in the inbox, NOISE → archived. Standing backlogs
(`status: standing`) are fed by triage rather than derived from a design, one
per repo or domain subfolder, with every line dated at arrival and swept —
deleted, not checked — by the commit that completes it.

**Consequences** — The inbox changes meaning from "everything unprocessed" to
"everything that needs thought", which makes `/brain:plan` an occasional deep
operation instead of a toll gate, and keeps the inbox count meaningful as the
backlog. Per-item classification is required because real captures are mixed
dumps; per-note classification would misfile most of them. Two risks are
accepted and mitigated: a backlog can become the new landfill, so every line
is dated and lint warns past 30 days; and `/brain:execute`'s take-all-items
default would be dangerous on a queue of unrelated external side effects, so
`status: standing` forces explicit selection. Work now has two lifecycles to
keep straight, which is the price of not forcing everything through one.
